Kevin Tu

Accessible precision medicine.
Sustainable healthcare and science.

I work at the intersection of cancer research, medicine, and sustainability. My work is guided by two goals: developing accessible and affordable precision medicines, and decarbonizing our healthcare and science systems.

I am a medical student at the University of Maryland School of Medicine, a researcher in neuro-oncology at Duke University, and a Provost Climate Health Fellow at Johns Hopkins University. Previously, I studied the breast cancer microenvironment as a Churchill Scholar at the University of Cambridge. I earned degrees in Physiology and Neurobiology and Economics at the University of Maryland, College Park.

My research connects tumor biology and computational analysis to better treatment strategies, from immune biomarkers to therapeutic vulnerabilities in cancer. Alongside this work, I founded Sustainabli to reduce laboratory energy waste, lead the International Fume Hood Challenge, and study the health impacts of waste incineration in Baltimore. I also work to improve access to care through telemedicine research and community harm reduction. See my featured publications below.

        Research

        I want advances in science to improve health while reducing the resources required to deliver them. My work spans three connected areas:

        Developing precision medicines

        Using cancer genomics, tumor microenvironment profiles, and experimental models to identify biomarkers and treatment vulnerabilities. I am especially interested in translating these findings into accessible, affordable approaches for patients with glioblastoma and other cancers.

        Improving access to care

        Studying how telemedicine can improve sepsis care and where social vulnerability limits its use. My work in community harm reduction and science education shares the same aim: making knowledge and effective care more widely available.

        Decarbonizing healthcare and science

        Developing technology and policy to reduce laboratory energy waste, repurposing cancer mutations for green chemistry, and measuring the health burden of waste incineration. Through Sustainabli and I2SL, I turn research into practical tools for sustainable science.
